The Six Stars hypothesis atlas.

Six character-generated scientific-thinking personas supply competing interpretations and constructive questions. Their contributions are translated into measurable design requirements and experiments—not treated as independent empirical discoveries.

RESEARCH DESIGN / v0.4NONCLINICALUPDATED 13 NOV 2026

The original comparison scorecard

Original user-provided graphic · Six Stars v9 / Scientific Thinking Comparison.

Evidence classification: These numbers are subjective editorial scores assigned to historical scientists and LLM-generated characters. There is no validated blinded measurement establishing parity with Newton, Darwin, Einstein, Curie, Faraday, Turing or Shannon. The persona outputs are useful as hypothesis prompts, not biological observations or independent scientific testimony.

The supplied Atlas audit reports 132 rows, 95 nonempty responses and 37 blank fields. Some entries are echoed, duplicated, mixed or context-warning flagged. The v0.4 design preserves that provenance and does not silently repair them into valid assertions.

Editorial rubric categories

The poster labels eight dimensions: conceptual originality, systems thinking, causal reasoning, mathematical maturity, empirical grounding, falsifiability, cross-domain synthesis and scientific intuition. These dimensions can organize an internal hypothesis-review workshop, but the provided star scores do not come from a reproducible calibration study.

Historical comparison: influence, not equivalence

Newton, Darwin, Einstein, Marie Curie, Faraday, Turing and Shannon are shown as reference figures. Their achievements are independently documented scientific history; mapping them onto 1–10 subjective bins does not rank actual scientific achievement or validate the generated characters. The site uses historical reference ideas only as methodological prompts:

FigureResearch lens
NewtonExplicit laws, quantitative prediction and falsification
DarwinCompeting explanations, selection and adaptation over time
EinsteinOperational definitions, invariants and edge cases
Marie CurieIndependent empirical evidence and careful measurement
FaradayObservable interactions and measurement apparatus
TuringComputable procedures, controls and software baselines
ShannonInformation capacity, coding, uncertainty and noise
