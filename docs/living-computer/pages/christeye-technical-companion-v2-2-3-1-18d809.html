ChristEye Technical Companion

Learning, memory, regulation, and continuity

Brad Gearon ChristEye, We Develop Software LLC Version 2.2.3 | 8 November 2026

Companion to The ChristEye Framework of Knowledge and Understanding Inquiry method manuscript v1.3.3 dated 8 November 2026

Purpose

This companion supports the ChristEye inquiry method with biological and physical evidence, operational definitions, candidate models, and reusable assessment records. The method connects support, retained organization, recruitment, expression, and consequences for affected beings.

The reference catalogues preserve all 96 Heart functions, all 56 Living Table entries, 63 molecular components, and 24 cellular and tissue components. Original overview images accompany readable tables. The energy extension distinguishes retained response organization from stored energy and specifies the accounting required for a proposed conversion advantage.

Version 2.2 adds Tree as a semantic search cue, a nested tree and helix hypothesis, typed structural measurements, and prediction H17. The indexing proposal and Version 2.1 community-scale Sentrification extension are retained. Section 5 and Appendices C, E, F, and G distinguish reported findings, literature synthesis, preliminary evidence, and new proposals. No new biological experiment or decoded memory is reported.

Coordinated implementation record dated 8 November 2026: conceptual analysis v1.3.3, technical companion v2.2.3 and patent draft v0.5.4. Appendix G records an executed bounded workflow, a deterministic noun–verb candidate extension and their limits. No new biological results, universal problem-solving capability or comparative superiority are reported.

Evidence status

A published result is reported with its preparation or species and measured endpoint. An author-defined term specifies how ChristEye organizes a question. A proposed mechanism adds a causal connection that remains to be tested. A model equation specifies relationships and assumptions; it does not acquire empirical status merely by being written mathematically. A disputed result is accompanied by the relevant methodological disagreement. These distinctions apply to individual claims, including claims within the same paragraph.

The E and P marks in the Heart poster are retained as source labels. E does not mean that every mechanism or every proposed metric on that tile has been validated. The Living Table's D, C, and L marks describe proposed context-dependent host effects. They are not intrinsic moral properties, clinical classifications, or a common physical scale. The readable catalogues give each entry an ordinary measurement interpretation.

2 Operational vocabulary and continuity

The framework becomes testable when its terms name a defined role in a specified system. Soul identifies the whole whose continuity is being considered. Knowledge identifies available information or acquired relationships. Understanding identifies appropriate use of those relationships. Roots identifies retained organization supporting later use. These roles are separable: a viable organism may be asleep, impaired, unable to communicate, or unfamiliar with a task while retaining its standing in care. [1]

Continuity at different scales

Continuity requires a declared level. A cell can replace many molecules while maintaining its organization. A bacterial cell can die during lysis while a phage lineage expands. A learned performance can disappear temporarily while the organism remains viable. A molecule changing isomeric state preserves some aspects of composition while changing configuration. None of these observations supplies one interchangeable definition of biological death, memory loss, chemical reaction, or particle annihilation. [1]

What rooting would add

A rooted state must outlast the immediate input and contribute to later function. A stronger claim requires appropriate use after delay and under changed conditions. The hierarchy of claims is useful: persistent state, history-dependent response, learned association, flexible transfer, and semantic or generative competence. Evidence at one level does not automatically establish every later level. The framework can be useful if it makes those distinctions easier to measure.

3 Recruitment, training, and the functions of the Heart

Learning stages and observable outcomes

Encoding, consolidation, retrieval, expression, and updating answer different questions. Encoding concerns acquiring a relationship during experience. Consolidation concerns changes that support its persistence. Retrieval concerns access to a retained relationship. Expression is the observed response, which also depends on perception, motivation, context and motor capacity. Updating requires a lasting change after renewed experience. A molecular intervention affecting one stage should not be described as controlling all five.

The proposed recruitment sequence is that a cue and current context interact with retained neuronal and astrocytic organization, producing a selected pattern of activity and an observable response. Astrocytes may participate in selection, permissive support, retained state, and later stabilization. The model permits reciprocal neuronal-glial influence. It does not require every memory to begin with an astrocyte signal or a conscious desire to recall. Spontaneous and cue-driven responses remain possible. [4, 5, 6]

Welfare and interpreting nonresponse

The 96 Heart entries form a functional observation catalogue. They are not 96 anatomically localized engrams. Nonresponse may reflect absent learning, inaccessible learning, competing needs, pain, sensory difficulty, fatigue, or a choice not to participate. A welfare-sensitive assessment records response opportunity, voluntary participation and distress alongside performance. The Holy Heart duty is active: investigate the unmet need and follow through on practicable support. It does not make compliance a measure of worth. [1]

Training a Wind

The author's example of daily language practice followed by work in a French café or kitchen separates rehearsal from situated use. Practice can establish sound, word, and action relationships; a work setting can demand listening under noise, responding to unfamiliar speakers, coordinating actions, and repairing misunderstandings. In the framework, a trained Wind is the resulting coordinated capability. The example supplies a proposed assessment setting rather than a controlled finding about a particular application or a guaranteed timetable. The earlier 2,000-hour phrase is a cultural reference, not a biological threshold. [1, 7]

An assessment should sample unpracticed utterances, delayed retention, comprehension without imitation, and appropriate action when surface cues vary. Improved speed can accompany reduced cognitive load, but accuracy, flexibility and error correction must remain visible. A fluent response may be highly practiced and still fail when a task changes. Rooting should therefore be assessed across several outcomes rather than through hours or output volume alone.

A dog's name and the role of reward

In the author's dog example, a sound is paired with a valued outcome. The proposed interpretation is that reward-related learning makes the sound worth attending to and changes the probability of orientation or approach. To distinguish this from general food anticipation, the relevant questions include whether the dog discriminates its name from similar sounds, responds across speakers and contexts, and retains the association when immediate food cues are absent. These are conceptual comparisons, not evidence that one behavior reveals an entire engram.

Evidence for animal word processing extends beyond simple approach behavior. Boros and colleagues found a canine EEG mismatch response when a familiar object word was followed by a nonmatching object; the effect was stronger for words owners reported as better known. This supports an interpretation involving expectations about familiar referents in the tested dogs. It does not establish identical language capacities in every animal species or human-like grammar. The source thesis also includes cat-name discrimination and canine lexical-processing studies, with the latter's published erratum retained in the references. [8, 9, 10, 11]

Community-scale Holy Heart and Sentrification

The main manuscript extends the Holy Heart duty beyond formal care relationships through Sentrification. In this companion, Sentrification is an author-defined community-scale inquiry term for situations in which an establishment or institution principally serves one population while people in the surrounding community with fewer resources remain under-considered. It does not classify commerce, wealth, specialization, or service to a chosen customer group as intrinsically harmful. It asks whether affected non-users and neighbors are represented in the decision record, what consequences they experience, and which practicable responses remain available. [1]

A minimal bookkeeping relation is D = F(P, V, U, N, C), where P is the stated purpose, V the viability constraints, U the intended users, N affected neighbors or non-users, and C observed or reasonably foreseeable consequences. This is not a calibrated social law. It is a completeness check: omitting N makes it impossible for the record to show whether nearby needs or burdens were considered. Reports of welcome or exclusion are evidence about experience, while claims about motives or systematic deprivation require separate evidence.

The Holy Heart application is active rather than merely prohibitive. The relevant question is not only whether an institution violated a rule, but whether visible suffering, confusion, exclusion, or unmet need was noticed and whether a responsible opportunity to help was ignored. The same boundary discipline used elsewhere applies: identify who is affected, distinguish kindness from material provision, respect consent and lawful authority, and do not infer moral character from one outcome.

For technical assessment, a Sentrification record should name the setting, intended users, affected non-users, resources or access conditions, observed experiences, available options, constraints, and consequences over a stated interval. Its proposed analytical value is weakened if it merely duplicates an existing stakeholder analysis without improving completeness, clarity, transfer, or decision quality.

4 Neuronal and astrocytic organization

A distributed substrate for learned response

ChristEye uses Knowledge for information or acquired relationships available to a learner and Understanding for the capacity to use them appropriately. The technical question is how these functions are implemented across changing cells and connections. An experimental engram denotes a physical trace associated with learning; activity-tagged neurons are one way to obtain access to part of that trace. Brain-wide mapping in mice supports distributed neuronal ensembles whose contributions to recall differ across regions. A single tagged population should consequently be described as a component of an engram network rather than a complete recording of everything perceived during an event. [12]

Storage, availability, and performance require separate observations. In a mouse model of experimentally induced amnesia, direct activation of learning-tagged neurons recovered a conditioned response when ordinary cues failed. This supports a distinction between retained capacity and natural access under those conditions. It does not license the assumption that every inaccessible human memory remains intact. For the present framework, a failure to respond should prompt competing explanations: loss of a learned relationship, reduced cue access, altered motivation, impaired sensory processing, or failure of the action system. [13]

Growth also has several measurable meanings. Mouse motor learning can produce new dendritic spines and preferentially stabilize a subset. A more recent electron-microscopy study found that learning-tagged hippocampal axons expanded their connections through multisynaptic boutons, with locally altered synapses and organelles. Those boutons did not selectively connect only to other initially coactivated neurons. The latter study provides a detailed post-learning structural snapshot, rather than longitudinal proof of when each connection formed. Ensemble membership, synaptic number, synaptic strength, astrocytic coverage, and cell birth must therefore be recorded separately. [14, 15]

The proposed mesh is the organization of these interacting relationships. Its functional value is demonstrated by retained discrimination, transfer, flexibility, or calibrated action. A larger mesh could support a richer capability, but size alone cannot identify that capability. Conversely, learning can occur while average contact density remains similar if selected contacts change identity, strength, or participation. This makes the Heart chart an inventory of outcomes to explain, with the molecular and cellular measurements providing candidate mechanisms for those outcomes.

Astrocytic ensembles and the recruitment of learned networks

Learning-associated astrocytes provide an experimentally grounded entry point for the recruitment proposal. Williamson and colleagues identified hippocampal astrocytes expressing c-Fos after learning, found them associated with neuronal engrams, and showed that reactivating the tagged astrocytes could promote conditioned recall. Selective disruption of NFIA in this population impaired recall. These experiments support a functional contribution by selected astrocytes; they do not assign the complete memory or its subjective content to a single glial cell. [4]

A complementary mouse study by Dewa and colleagues identified astrocytic changes that persisted across days. Initial fear experience primed responsiveness to noradrenaline; during later recall, local engram-related signals and noradrenergic input converged on the primed population. This recruitment included IGFBP2-related signaling and supported later memory stability. The important distinction is between a lasting change in responsiveness, the signal that re-engages the cells, and the subsequent stabilization outcome. The mechanism is reciprocal and does not require astrocytes to initiate every act of remembering. [5]

Two 2026 studies widen the account. Bukalo and colleagues combined astrocytic manipulations with neuronal measurements in mouse basolateral amygdala, finding that astrocytic calcium signaling supported representations involved in fear retrieval and extinction. Senne and colleagues observed ordered astrocytic calcium-event sequences in dorsal CA1 during conditioning and their recurrence in the conditioned context. The sequence result is observational: related arousal and behavioral transitions remain possible contributors. Together, these studies distinguish causal participation in a learned response from the observation of temporally organized activity. [16, 17]

The working ChristEye model is a distributed recruitment process. An astrocytic signal can help change the availability of an associated neuronal configuration; neuronal activity and neuromodulatory input can in turn recruit or modify astrocytes. Calling this an invitation to assemble the learned team describes the proposed function. Its source, recipient, timing, and information content must be identified for each circuit. The author uses replay for experiential retrieval. For measurement, distinguish cue-triggered reinstatement of a configuration, temporally structured neural replay, and behavioral recall. Reactivation alone establishes renewed participation; an ordered relationship to previous activity needs a sequence measure. Neither behavioral success nor ensemble activity by itself establishes accurate reinstatement of every sensory or emotional detail.

Molecular signatures and the unit of inference

Sun and colleagues reported spatially associated neuron-astrocyte transcriptional changes after fear learning, including Igfbp2-related findings. Their 2025 statistical exchange is essential context. Mukamel and Yu reported that accounting for multiple comparisons and correlations among cells from the same animal removed significant differential-expression findings. Sun and colleagues disputed the criticism and defended their methodology. The expression signature should therefore enter the companion as contested evidence, alongside the distinct perturbation results, rather than as an established dictionary translating RNA into remembered events. [18, 19, 20]

The relevant experimental unit is usually the independently treated animal or preparation. Thousands of cells from a few animals increase resolution within those animals; they do not automatically supply thousands of independent experimental replicates. A predictive analysis should preserve donor, animal, batch, brain region, cell type, time, and condition labels. Evaluation should leave whole independent units out, and adjustment for multiple testing should match the claims being made. These requirements are especially important when a composite molecular score is named a knowledge state.

Even a reproducible signature can track stress, activity, cell composition, or recovery rather than the specific learned content. Evidence for a knowledge-related molecular state becomes stronger when its relation to the target behavior survives those alternatives, predicts later retention in independent individuals, and changes appropriately under a causally informative perturbation. A useful negative finding narrows the proposed code: failure of transcript abundance to discriminate memories does not imply that the circuit retained no information in its connectivity, protein localization, or activity.

5 Molecules, support, and network integration

CaMKII and the proposed record of support

CaMKII links calcium-dependent signaling to changes at synapses. Its catalytic and regulatory regions are coupled to an oligomerization hub; the neuronal alpha and beta forms have related but distinct interactions. Calcium-calmodulin, autophosphorylation, binding to partners such as GluN2B, and synaptic localization describe different properties of the complex. Imaging during single-spine potentiation detected a brief activation episode, while the synaptic consequences endured longer. A transient writing process and an enduring stored consequence are therefore compatible possibilities. [21, 22]

The maintenance mechanism remains an active experimental issue. Chen and colleagues concluded that CaMKII autophosphorylation and GluN2B binding could sustain their measured synaptic enhancement without phosphorylation of downstream targets. Rumian and colleagues separated induction, an intermediate expression phase, and later maintenance, finding a temporally restricted requirement for autonomous activity of GluN2B-bound CaMKII. These findings should be presented together because the preparations, interventions, and measured phases constrain their interpretation. Neither study decodes the content of a remembered event. [23, 24]

A 2026 analysis of the D135N tool mutation showed that it reduces GluN2B binding as well as eliminating detectable kinase activity. Thus, a perturbation intended to distinguish catalytic and structural functions can affect both. The published authors still considered their result compatible with a structural role in LTP induction. The correction concerns what an intervention isolates, and should not be recast as a general refutation or confirmation of every CaMKII-based memory model. [25]

The ChristEye Milk record can be formulated as a local state that carries consequences of prior activity or support into later plasticity. To become a testable mechanism, the record must name a variable: phosphorylation, conformation, binding occupancy, localization, or a downstream structural change. It must also specify the interval over which that variable persists and the response it predicts. A calcium-sensitive molecular state may help determine how a synapse changes without recording every feature of the environment that produced it. The hypothesis becomes more informative when it predicts selective later effects beyond those explained by general excitability or health.

Arc dynamin and selective updating

Arc provides several distinct mechanisms connecting activity with subsequent synaptic function. Okuno and colleagues found preferential association of Arc with inactive CaMKIIβ and selective Arc accumulation at relatively inactive synapses, accompanying reduced surface AMPA receptors. This inverse-tagging mechanism offers a concrete example of local activity history guiding where a shared plasticity protein acts. A separate study linked CaMKII phosphorylation of Arc to changes in Arc oligomerization and synaptic weakening. Localization and assembly are different control points; they should not be merged into one unspecified transfer of data. [26, 27]

Chowdhury and colleagues demonstrated interactions involving Arc, dynamin 2, and particular endophilin isoforms, with effects on AMPA-receptor endocytosis. Shepherd and colleagues linked Arc to homeostatic synaptic scaling. These findings explain why more Arc is not a universal measure of greater knowledge: changing receptor availability can serve selective weakening, compensation, or other forms of circuit adjustment. The relevant measurement is the effect at identified synapses within a defined learning phase. [28, 29]

Dynamin supplies membrane-remodeling machinery. It uses GTP-dependent assembly to participate in membrane fission; structural and reconstitution studies describe the relevant domains and dynamic assemblies. Arc can enhance the assembly and activity of some dynamin isoforms in biochemical assays, while dynamin 3-Homer coupling helps position postsynaptic endocytic zones and maintain receptor cycling. These observations make receptor renewal a defensible meaning of refreshing a functional configuration. They do not identify a dynamin polymer as the stored template of an experience. [30, 31, 32, 33]

Arc also has a molecular-communication role. Pastuzyn and colleagues demonstrated capsid-like assembly, RNA packaging, and intercellular transfer involving Arc mRNA in experimental systems. A transferred RNA can change the recipient’s molecular capacity without conveying a learned association. ChristEye can investigate whether such processes help coordinate local plasticity, provided it specifies the cargo, receiving cell, spatial destination, and later function. Information transfer at the level of a particular learned relationship requires evidence that separates transferred content from a general change in growth, responsiveness, or ability to learn. [34]

Tree architecture helical scaffolds and the local memory interface

The author proposes a nested Tree of Knowledge architecture in which dendritic and astrocytic branching defines higher-order organization, while branched and helical molecular assemblies contribute to the local structural state of synaptic nodes. Here Tree of Knowledge names ChristEye’s structural hypothesis and the semantic cue that prompted a literature search. It does not identify a biblical object with a biological structure. The central question is whether a learned relationship leaves a reproducible structural signature across branch, spine, scaffold, and astrocytic interface that predicts later selective recall beyond general activity and plasticity.

The following layers distinguish measured components from the proposed connection among them. The full sequence has not been demonstrated in one experiment. Its useful output is a set of candidate measurements and competing explanations, with preserved scientific identity, units, scale, preparation, and evidence status.

Layer 1 Dendritic trees and branches

The 2023 review The dendritic engram develops a branch-centered account of memory using experimental and computational literature on clustered synapses, nonlinear dendritic integration, and local plasticity. Its proposal that dendritic branches can act as elementary engram units is a theoretical synthesis, not proof that every branch stores a discrete memory. [121]

Reviews describe structural plasticity and actin-related remodeling, but do not establish that each leaflet carries a memory-specific datum. [122, 123] In adult mice, astrocyte-specific Ank2 deletion impaired remote memory and reduced contacts with engram neurons; the study connects astrocytic morphology with memory persistence within its tested conditions. F-actin contains two protofilaments arranged as a right-handed helix. Spine actin networks can contain Arp2/3-mediated branches; filament helicity and network branching are distinct structural properties. [125, 126] Basu and Lamprecht review a proposal in which preservation of actin branch organization helps maintain spine morphology despite molecular turnover. That persistence mechanism remains a model rather than a decoded memory code. [127]

Layer 4 Shank and Homer scaffolds

Baron and colleagues showed that the isolated rat Shank3/ProSAP2 SAM domain can assemble into helical fibers and sheets in structural and biochemical preparations. [128] The later Caskin1 paper describes six SAM domains per turn for the previously characterized Shank polymers; its own tandem-SAM Caskin1 assembly has eight domains per turn. These are different proteins and results. [130] Hayashi and colleagues demonstrated a mesh-like Homer–Shank assembly and a role for long-Homer tetramerization in synaptic organization. [129]

These observations make Shank–Homer a specific candidate scaffold for investigation. Six domains per turn is an assembly property of the characterized system, not a universal in vivo PSD geometry or a memory symbol. The existence of a polymeric scaffold does not establish that its pitch or symmetry varies with learned content. Molecular isoform, construct, assembly conditions, preparation and assay resolution must remain attached to every structural claim.

Layer 5 Local remodeling and receptor renewal

The candidate relationship connects PSD/Shank scaffolding, Homer, dynamin-3 and the endocytic zone. Dyn3–Homer coupling helps maintain local endocytic positioning and AMPA-receptor recycling in tested neuronal preparations. Separately, Arc enhances dynamin-2 and dynamin-3 polymerization and GTPase activity in biochemical assays. [30, 31, 129] Taken together, these findings motivate a local remodeling or refresh interpretation. They do not show that dynamin contains an archive or that every component participates in a single ordered, memory-specific pathway.

Layer 6 Localized RNA and the reference hypothesis

Dynes and Steward used fluorescent mRNA reporters in cultured neurons to show that transcripts containing the Arc 3′ untranslated region could dock at spine-base microdomains; localization persisted with translation inhibition. The paper was published in 2012, although its PMC manuscript became available in 2017. [131] This supplies a localization mechanism relevant to the author’s reference-object question. It does not establish an experiential database address, unique memory identifier, or the meaning of the localized RNA.

A proposed localization record Rs should identify the transcript or reporter construct, spine, three-dimensional position, dwell time, activity history, assay, and uncertainty. Spatial targeting, translation, protein action and subsequent recall are separate outcomes. A reference role is supported only to the extent that the tested localization and consequence are supported; it cannot inherit a semantic addressing claim from computing terminology.

Layer 7 Molecular packets and extracellular vesicles

The established Arc capsid and RNA-transfer studies remain the foundation of this layer. [34] A separate preprint by Sullivan and colleagues, indexed as an August 2026 version, reports IRSp53-dependent Arc vesicle biogenesis after LTP stimuli, release from actin-rich dendritic protrusions, and transfer associated with loss of surface AMPA receptors in neighboring dendrites. It is preliminary evidence, not a peer-reviewed result in this record. [132]

Represent a candidate packet by separately observed capsid state, cargo identity, membrane status, source, recipient, transport interval and measured consequence. An Arc capsid and an extracellular vesicle are not interchangeable objects; absence of membrane or cargo evidence must remain unresolved. Delivery of RNA or a change in receptor abundance does not establish delivery of a learned association.

Layer 8 CSF and extracellular transport

A human CSF enrichment study detected ACTB among proteins enriched in EV preparations. A separate multiple-sclerosis study identified DNM1 in CSF EV proteomics. DNM1 must not be substituted for the dynamin-2 or dynamin-3 findings above. The defensible extension is that related molecular material is detectable in extracellular CNS-fluid preparations. IGFBP2 IGF2BP2 Arc and the proposed indexing function

ChristEye proposes indexing as a distributed function with three linked roles: registering acquired relationships during learning, organizing the components recruited when an experience is recalled, and updating or stabilizing those relationships after use. The author assigns IGFBP2 a candidate coordinating role across these operations, in collaboration with IGF2BP2, Arc, and the participating network. In this proposal, to log where a relationship is stored means to establish a usable relationship among a cue or context and particular cellular, synaptic, or subcellular states. It does not require an immutable address or one molecule containing the index. [3]

The molecular identities remain distinct. IGFBP2 is an IGF-binding protein with extracellular and signaling-related functions; IGF2BP2, also called IMP2, is an intracellular RNA-binding regulator; Arc is an activity-regulated protein with several synaptic and molecular-communication functions. IGF2 is a growth-factor ligand. Chen and colleagues reported IGF2-dependent enhancement of later retention after retrieval in rats, with requirements involving protein synthesis and Arc. That result concerns IGF2 and does not identify an IGFBP2-IGF2BP2 indexing pathway. [35, 36, 37]

Among the studies reviewed here, Dewa most directly supports the stabilization branch. Dewa and colleagues found experience-primed astrocytes that integrated local engram-related and noradrenergic signals during later recall; IGFBP2-associated signaling contributed to subsequent memory stability. The ensemble results discussed in Section 4 make astrocytic participation a concrete candidate mechanism, while leaving initial relationship registration and replay organization as additional questions. [4, 5] Khan and colleagues connected IGFBP2 with developmental circuit properties and learning performance. The IGFBP2-derived peptide JB2 altered plasticity and behavioral outcomes in preclinical preparations and a Shank3-related mouse model. These findings motivate work on enabling conditions; neither result assigns learned content to the peptide or establishes the full native protein's role at every learning stage. [35, 38]

IGF2BP2 supplies a separate candidate contribution through regulation of selected RNAs. Preitner and colleagues connected its RNA interactions with developing axon trajectories. Zhao and colleagues subsequently linked an IGF2BP2-Shox2 regulatory axis to hippocampal cell-state markers and learning or memory outcomes in a mouse tail-suspension model of aspects of microgravity, supported by cell assays. Their study extends the evidence beyond developmental guidance, but its measured outcomes do not demonstrate selection of a particular learned experience. IGF2BP2 is treated here as an RNA-binding regulator and m6A reader, not an enzyme that writes m6A modifications. [36, 39]

Arc offers candidate mechanisms through which a recruited configuration can be adjusted. Local activity history can influence Arc localization, Arc interacts with receptor-endocytic machinery, and Arc can package and transfer RNA in experimental systems. These are distinct operations, detailed earlier in this section. The indexing proposal asks whether their deployment helps preserve or revise the relationships needed to reconstruct a learned response. Evidence for RNA movement alone does not identify the learned relationship, if any, that movement conveys. [26, 28, 34]

The proposed collaboration therefore has to be specified at the level of a circuit, cellular compartment, and learning phase. Selectivity could arise from local activity, the identity of participating ensembles, and context, while IGFBP2-related signaling changes which interactions remain usable and IGF2BP2-related RNA regulation changes available molecular machinery. This is a functional hypothesis, not evidence that any of these proteins directly reads the meaning of an experience. An extracellular influence, intracellular RNA regulation, and receptor updating may act in parallel or through intermediate processes. Fast recruitment and slower transcriptional or structural changes must be distinguished. A shared behavioral effect or overlapping expression is insufficient to establish a particular causal link.

Replay also requires a declared endpoint. The author uses the term for bringing an experience forward again. Cue-triggered reinstatement measures recurrence of an identified activity configuration; temporally structured neural replay additionally measures its ordered relationship to prior activity; behavioral recall measures task expression. One cannot infer the second from behavioral success alone. The stage comparisons below distinguish initial association registration, current recruitment, and later updating or stabilization. A result confined to one stage can support that branch without establishing the others.

Ionic and metabolic support for a usable circuit

Electrical signaling depends on maintained ionic gradients and their local regulation. Potassium channels permit conductance, while the sodium-potassium ATPase consumes metabolic energy to sustain gradients. Larsen and colleagues distinguished mechanisms contributing to extracellular potassium handling in hippocampal preparations; the involvement of Kir4.1, pumps, and NKCC1 depended on the phase and preparation measured. Potassium should consequently be modeled as a regulated physiological variable, with excessive or inadequate extracellular accumulation capable of changing responsiveness. It is not a quantity that can be increased monotonically to add learning. [40]

Glutamate uptake helps restrict excitation in space and time. Astroglial transporter disruption has been associated with failed clearance and excitotoxic injury. Calcium, magnesium, and chloride also have distinct roles: magnesium influences NMDA-channel gating, while chloride transport contributes to the developmental effects of GABA. These mechanisms cannot be collapsed into a single beneficial-ion score. Cell identity, membrane potential, location, developmental stage, and transport direction determine the physiological effect. [41, 42, 43]

Metabolic exchange supplies another part of the model. Suzuki and colleagues implicated astrocyte-associated glycogen metabolism and lactate transport in long-term memory formation in rats. Rouach and colleagues showed that astroglial coupling can distribute metabolites and support hippocampal transmission under metabolic challenge. These results support provision as an enabling condition for learned function. They leave separate questions about the relative contribution of different fuels, signaling effects of metabolites, and the specific information retained by the circuit. [44, 45]

Milk can organize these support variables if each retains its units and physiological range. Honey can organize recognition, guidance, and stabilization variables if the relevant recipient and mechanism are specified. A molecule may serve both roles in different settings. Adequate support should be evaluated through preserved viability, selective responsiveness, plasticity, and performance; a larger calcium signal, more lactate, or stronger electrical activity alone does not establish improved Understanding. This formulation allows supportive conditions to be investigated without turning the framework’s vocabulary into a prescribed mixture.

Assembling refining and maintaining the mesh

Astrocytes contribute multiple extracellular operations to synapse development. Thrombospondins promoted structural contacts that required additional signals for mature postsynaptic function. Hevin can bridge particular neurexin and neuroligin isoforms in developing thalamocortical circuits. Glypicans 4 and 6 promoted functional excitatory synapses and GluA1 receptor recruitment in neuronal preparations. Glia-derived cholesterol supported synaptogenesis in purified neuronal cultures. These studies distinguish contact assembly, receptor maturation, and material supply, with experimental scope tied to their preparations. [46, 47, 48, 49]

Growth also depends on selective retention and removal. Astrocytic MEGF10/MERTK pathways and microglial complement-related engulfment have been implicated in developmental synapse elimination. The two cell classes and pathways should remain distinct. Separately, preventing production of new oligodendrocytes impaired acquisition of a complex motor skill in mice. The communication infrastructure around a circuit can therefore participate in learning alongside synaptic modifications. The framework’s catalogue should include pruning and myelin-related adaptation as well as new contacts. [50, 51, 52]

Geometry and local state matter. In one experimental potentiation setting, withdrawal of perisynaptic astroglial processes increased glutamate spillover. Three-dimensional imaging also showed abundant localized astrocytic calcium events outside cell bodies. Thus, a whole-cell mean may conceal the signal relevant to a nearby synapse, and more astrocytic coverage is not universally synonymous with better function. The companion should record the measured compartment, the timescale, and the relation to neighboring synapses before assigning a process to growth or stabilization. [53, 54]

Rooting Understanding can consequently be tested as selective persistence of useful relationships through ongoing renewal. Candidate measurements include contact identity, receptor distribution, transmission reliability, inhibition, local metabolic support, and performance after a delay. A successful model should explain why some changes endure and others are reversed. It should also permit continued learning: a perfectly frozen network could preserve an old response while failing to accommodate a changed task. Stable capability and molecular turnover are compatible features of a living system.

Stem cells neural crest and learning after integration

Developmental potential must be specified for the cell population concerned. Neural crest cells arise in an embryo and generate multiple lineage-associated derivatives; a neural crest population is not synonymous with the totipotent cell from which an embryo develops. Basch and colleagues identified an early specification role for Pax7 in chick neural crest. Neural stem cells, pluripotent stem cells, and neural crest-derived populations consequently require separate descriptions of origin, permitted fates, and the target circuit. [55]

Berg and colleagues showed that human neural stem cells directly programmed from adult blood could mature after transplantation into immunodeficient adult mice. Graft-derived neurons exhibited electrophysiological properties and received synaptic inputs, supporting structural and functional integration. The experiment demonstrates a route from a non-neural donor source to participating neural cells. It did not examine recovery of the blood donor’s acquired knowledge, and its host preparation limits generalization to ordinary immune compatibility. [56]

Human adult neurogenesis remains a separate question. Sorrells and colleagues reported very limited adult hippocampal neurogenesis in their samples; Dumitru and colleagues subsequently identified proliferating adult neural progenitors using molecular and spatial approaches. The more recent result supports the presence of progenitors, while leaving their production rates, maturation trajectories, and contribution to particular memories incompletely resolved. Network expansion should therefore not be equated with a rule that each learned capability produces new neurons. [57, 58]

For ChristEye, joining a network should be decomposed into survival, appropriate identity, anatomical contact, functional communication, participation in a learned ensemble, and useful behavioral contribution. These are cumulative questions, not interchangeable success labels. An incoming cell may acquire new organization through interactions with its host; it need not arrive carrying the host’s experience. A proposed transfer of donor knowledge makes the stronger prediction that a specific learned relationship is available without ordinary recipient training, and must be distinguished from improved growth or accelerated learning.

RNA localization and reconstruction of a learned capability

RNA contributes to durable change through its identity, abundance, location, timing, and translation. Miller and colleagues disrupted dendritic targeting of CaMKIIα mRNA while retaining its coding sequence, impairing stabilization of synaptic plasticity and memory consolidation. Correct sequence alone was therefore insufficient for the measured function when spatial delivery changed. This is directly relevant to the proposal that an infant or newly integrated cell might replay molecular instructions: a useful reconstruction would need the receiving architecture and delivery context as well as the available RNA. [59]

The RNA-replay proposal should distinguish at least three possible outcomes. A recipient may become more viable or plastic; it may learn more efficiently when trained; or it may acquire a specific relation present in the donor’s experience. The first two outcomes can arise without transfer of memory content. The third requires a content-sensitive behavioral comparison, independent controls for ordinary sensory learning, and evidence relating the transferred state to the recipient’s expression. No single transcript inventory identifies the causal sequence by which a circuit acquired its organization.

A reconstructed capability need not reproduce every original synapse to preserve a learned relationship. The meaningful criterion is functional correspondence under a defined set of cues and demands, including appropriate failure when information is insufficient. This permits a substantive research program around network joining while keeping molecular availability distinct from semantic understanding. The same principle applies to a trained Wind: its proposed biological referent is a retained, generative competence that can transfer and adapt, rather than a fixed count of practice hours or an isolated molecule carrying a whole discipline.

6 Microbial regulation, peptides, and host conditions

Biological entities and forms of information retention

ChristEye uses continuity, support, and response selection to compare systems at several biological scales. The comparison becomes technically useful when the entity, retained state, incoming influence, and observable response are specified separately. A bacterial cell, a phage particle, a phage genome within a bacterium, and an animal carrying a microbial community are different experimental subjects. An outcome that preserves one of these subjects can impose a cost on another. The companion therefore records both the immediate recipient of a signal and the larger organism or community whose function is being assessed.

Three microbial examples distinguish different meanings of a biological record. In Streptococcus thermophilus, acquisition and experimental alteration of CRISPR spacers changed resistance to particular phages. Sequence similarity connected the retained record to the specificity of a later response. This is an example of sequence-associated immune discrimination. [63]

In Escherichia coli, Goy and colleagues related changes in membrane-protein methylation to sensory adaptation. Prior chemical exposure changed the modification state and subsequent responsiveness. Such a biochemical state retains aspects of recent stimulation without representing a narrated episode. [64]

Arbitrium provides a third form: an extracellular signal accumulated through local biological activity can influence later infection outcomes. Here the relevant information concerns ecological history and the regulatory state of the recipient. These examples support comparing information-bearing functions across scales while keeping their physical implementations distinct. [65]

For the learning thesis, the distinction prevents an ambiguous use of the word memory. A molecular modification, an immune sequence record, a persistent viral association, and the recall of an animal experience may all depend on history, but they require different readouts. Rooting Understanding is an author-defined account of acquiring a durable ability to respond; demonstrating it in any proposed system requires an operational task, persistence over time, and appropriate use under changed conditions.

Arbitrium and the selection of developmental programs

In the temperate Bacillus phages studied by Erez and colleagues, a phage-encoded AimP precursor gives rise to a communication peptide. After uptake into a bacterial recipient, the signal interacts with AimR and changes downstream regulation, favoring lysogeny under the tested conditions. ChristEye describes lytic development and lysogeny as competing instructions within a coupled regulatory system. This terminology concerns alternative patterns of gene activity. [65]

The downstream mechanism is not uniform across arbitrium systems. In phage phi3T, AimR acts as a transcriptional antiterminator that enables expression of aimX from the aimP promoter. AimX is a protein interacting with phage and host factors, including the MazE-MazF toxin-antitoxin system. AimX and YosL inhibit MazF; AimX also interferes with the phage factor phi3T_93, which otherwise favors MazF release from MazE. These opposing interactions connect signaling to developmental outcome. [66]

A paired study showed that controlled MazF ribonuclease activity could favor lysogeny while preserving bacterial viability. Lytic transcripts and the phage repressor transcript differed in susceptibility to this regulation. Thus the functional effect of a component conventionally called a toxin depended on its activity and molecular targets in that system. This is a particularly useful example for assigning Milk and Honey roles according to measured consequences. [67]

Kabel and colleagues identified an extended aimX-aimC-aimL architecture in approximately three quarters of the arbitrium systems in their survey. In this architecture, AimX is an antirepressor protein that interferes with AimC oligomerization and DNA binding, permitting expression of aimL and other lytic genes. The result identifies a prevalent mechanism among surveyed arbitrium systems; the fraction does not describe all bacteriophages or all viruses. [68]

Arbitrium also operates after a prophage has become established. Aframian and colleagues found that lysogens continued producing and sensing signals, with effects on DNA-damage-associated induction and nearby infections. The integration of communication and damage signals differed between phages. Establishment of lysogeny and continued restraint of lytic development are therefore related but separable endpoints. [69]

These findings warrant a structured regulatory model with signal production, recipient recognition, intracellular processing, and a state-dependent outcome. They do not justify assigning one invariant mechanism to the entire category. In a technical record, AimP should be distinguished from the AimR receptor and from downstream regulatory proteins or transcripts. The biological identity of each component takes precedence over a convenient software analogy.

Peptide selectivity and the scope of reprogramming

The demonstrated peptide effect is modulation of an existing regulatory balance. Because lysis and lysogeny are alternative outcomes of a coupled system, changing the balance can alter the probability of both. Independent redesign of each program, control of every stage after commitment, and stable conversion to a beneficial association are additional hypotheses. The companion uses program selection for the demonstrated class of effect and reserves program rewriting for evidence that the underlying instructions themselves have changed.

Selectivity is receptor dependent and can include crosstalk. Gallego-del-Sol and colleagues showed that compatible non-cognate AimP signals can interact with other AimR receptors and change infection or induction outcomes. A signal therefore need not be exclusive to one phage identity, but cross-recognition still depends on the molecular system. [70]

Communication also does not establish common benefit. Manley and colleagues found that signals from one phage could encourage early lysogeny in another, benefiting the emitter while imposing costs on the responder. The relevant assessment includes each participant and the surrounding ecology. [71]

ChristEye accordingly proposes targeted signaling as a research direction with explicit recipients and objectives. A candidate interaction would need demonstrated recognition, an interpretable downstream effect, and a defined benefit at the chosen host scale. The word targeted concerns biological selectivity; it is not satisfied merely by applying a molecule and observing a general change in growth or activity. Effects attributed to communication must be distinguished from nonspecific impairment, altered nutrition, and changes in the abundance of the participants.

Peptide regulation of learning and memory retrieval

The bridge to the learning thesis is regulation of access and plasticity. ChristEye proposes that peptide signals can function as regulatory code by changing the conditions in which learned responses become available, expressed, or strengthened. The receiving network contributes its pre-existing connections and experience. The proposal can therefore be evaluated without presuming that a peptide sequence contains the semantic content of an experience.

One direct retrieval result concerns neuropeptide Y signaling. Méndez-Couz and colleagues found that blocking hippocampal Y2 receptors improved spatial recall in previously trained rats and altered regional metabolic readouts. This supports a contribution of peptide-receptor regulation to retrieval. Because the manipulation was receptor blockade, the result also shows why increasing a signal cannot automatically be equated with improving performance. [72]

A complementary result concerns reinforcement after recall. Chen and colleagues reported that IGF2 given after retrieval enhanced subsequent retention in rats. The enhancement required IGF2 receptors, new protein synthesis, and Arc. This provides a specific connection between a polypeptide signal and mechanisms involved in strengthening an activated memory. IGF2, IGFBP2, and IGF2BP2 are distinct molecular entities and should retain separate entries in the technical glossary. [37]

A 2026 mouse study separated contributions of oxytocin-expressing projections to social-memory encoding and retrieval in a sleep-deprivation model. Manipulating the hypothalamus-to-CA2 and hypothalamus-to-prelimbic pathways produced stage-dependent effects. Because pathway manipulation can affect more than peptide release alone, this study supports the circuit-level claim most directly. Establishing exclusive peptide causality requires a corresponding receptor-level or otherwise discriminating result. [73]

These studies address different time points and outcomes. Acquisition concerns learning from an encounter; retrieval concerns access to an existing learned response; post-retrieval strengthening concerns later persistence. The same behavioral score can be influenced by motivation, sensory discrimination, arousal, movement, and the opportunity to respond. A claim about Understanding should therefore include retained performance and appropriate use in new circumstances, with those alternative explanations considered.

A content-selective claim would be stronger than a general improvement in recall. It would require a reproducible relationship between a specified signal and a particular learned representation, beyond overall activation or performance. At present, the studies summarized here motivate research on regulation of learned function. They do not demonstrate the insertion of an unlearned discipline, autobiographical memory transfer, or a universal peptide language shared with phages.

Viral sanctuary and host compatible association

The Viral Sanctuary proposal contains several objectives that need separate technical descriptions: supporting a transplanted cell, maintaining a beneficial symbiont, intercepting a virus before productive infection, and establishing a relationship with reciprocal benefit. A successful result for one objective cannot stand in for another. The companion treats these as distinct candidate functions within a common concern for continued host health.

A receptor decoy exemplifies interception. Greve and colleagues found that soluble ICAM-1 could neutralize rhinovirus, but much of the effect was reversible and depended on the receptor remaining present. Irreversible particle changes also occurred, yet were not the main neutralization mechanism. Binding, sustained neutralization, and loss of infectivity therefore require separate evidence. [74]

An engineered or grafted cell proposed as a source of support would introduce additional questions about identity, survival, integration, and its effects on the surrounding tissue. A proposed neural crest or stem-cell contribution would need an identified binding interface, secreted product, niche effect, or integration function. The claim that a cellular niche could maintain a benign viral association remains an individual hypothesis about a specified host and agent, separate from successful neuronal integration.

Lysogeny illustrates why persistence alone is insufficient. In a simplified mouse intestinal ecosystem, De Paepe and colleagues found that lambda prophage carriage could burden its bacterial host through reactivation, while providing an indirect advantage when susceptible competitors were present. The association had context-dependent consequences. Preserving a bacterium is also not automatically beneficial to an animal or human carrying it. [75]

For ChristEye, a supportive association must therefore be defined through observable function at every relevant host scale. Where continued viral presence is part of the proposed objective, the record should distinguish immediate host survival, durable compatibility, the possibility of renewed destructive activity, and any reciprocal benefit. A non-permissive decoy and a permissive long-term association are different designs, even when both are described using the Sanctuary vocabulary.

Microbial ecology and the conditions for neural function

The learning environment includes influences outside the nervous system. These influences can be studied as possible modifiers of neural state without treating a microbial community as an alternative store of an animal’s knowledge. The required connection is a chain of measurable events linking the community or its products to a host pathway and a defined neural or behavioral outcome.

Bravo and colleagues found that a particular Lactobacillus rhamnosus strain altered regional GABA-receptor mRNA and several stress-related measures in mice; the relevant effects were absent after vagotomy. The work supports a vagus-dependent contribution in that model. Its transcript measurements do not establish equivalent receptor-protein changes, and the findings cannot be assigned to all bacterial strains. [76]

Erny and colleagues found altered microglial maturation and immune responses in germ-free mice and after microbiota perturbation. Microbial short-chain fatty acids and host FFAR2 signaling contributed to the phenotype. This links microbial ecology to the state of brain immune cells, without requiring a claim that every metabolite acts directly on neurons. [77]

Ritz and colleagues reported that a transferred pre-stress virome preparation changed stress-associated behavioral, immune, microbial, and amygdala-expression outcomes in mice. This supports an effect of the transferred preparation under those conditions. It does not identify a single responsible phage, an arbitrium signal, or a route carrying the content of an animal memory. [78]

The proposed Milk and Honey categories can organize these observations by effect: conditions that sustain capacity, and influences that bias response selection. An explanatory model must then specify the mediating physiology. A behavioral change after a community intervention could reflect stress regulation, sickness, energy availability, altered sensory salience, or a learning process. Establishing which route explains the change is part of the central research problem rather than a detail to be inferred from the terminology.

Senescence and conditions that reinforce dysfunction

The four Milk and Honey categories distinguish the direction of an effect from its functional role. In the proposed vocabulary, Honey of Good denotes influences guiding repair or useful engagement; Milk of Good denotes conditions sustaining recovery and reliable function. Honey of Evil denotes influences that bias a defined system toward damaging outcomes; Milk of Evil denotes conditions that maintain or deepen that dysfunction. These are author-defined outcome classifications, with the recipient and observation period specified.

Bussian and colleagues provided model-specific causal evidence involving glial senescence. A tauopathy mouse model accumulated senescent astrocytes and microglia; preventing their accumulation preserved neurons and cognitive measures while reducing associated pathology. The result supports investigating adverse cellular environments as contributors to impaired function. It does not establish that all immune activity or all glial responses are harmful. [79]

A useful technical distinction separates an external influence, the recipient’s internal state, and the response it produces. A transient immune response, a persistent senescent state, and an observed failure of recall are different measurements. The proposed model predicts relationships among them; it should not collapse them into one latent category merely because each can accompany distress.

Accordingly, a restorative interpretation requires evidence that a change in the candidate influence precedes and explains a functional improvement. If the measured signal changes while the impaired response persists, the association may be incomplete or noncausal. If function improves without the proposed mediator changing, an alternative pathway may account for the result. The framework remains useful only when its assignments can be revised in response to such observations.

Soul continuity and the objective of rescue

Soul denotes the organized whole in the ChristEye vocabulary. For technical work, continuity must be assigned to a defined subject: the individual cell, a persistent genome, a replicating lineage, an integrated tissue, or the organism carrying that tissue. Soul death denotes loss of the specified continuing organization. A classification without the subject and time scale cannot distinguish successful propagation of a lineage from destruction of an individual host.

The rescue objective is therefore a prospective preservation or restoration of a specified function. A reduction in immediate destruction, retention of an organized association, and recovery of the larger host’s abilities are different possible achievements. Each should be named and measured directly. These functional definitions do not, by themselves, establish subjective experience in viral particles or settle philosophical questions about life and consciousness.

The Holy Heart Principle gives this research its care-oriented purpose: take the needs of affected organisms seriously, investigate conditions producing harm, and seek outcomes that preserve or restore useful living function. In a conflict between biological participants, the assessment must report whose interests and functions are served. An increase in the persistence of one participant cannot silently substitute for an improvement in the welfare of another.

The corresponding scientific contribution is a set of discriminating questions about regulation, support, compatibility, and retained capability. Rooting Understanding remains the proposed formation of a dependable capacity to respond. Molecular and microbial findings can identify conditions that support that capacity; demonstrating the capacity itself still requires an appropriate behavioral or functional task. A universal account would need additional evidence showing that the same formal relationships predict outcomes across independently studied systems.

A staged assessment

The first stage is definitional: make the entity, state variable, input, outcome and comparison explicit. The second is a reproducibility stage using available data and independent provenance checks. The third establishes whether a proposed variable provides information beyond simpler models. Causal work then tests a specific interaction or stage. Translation to education, healthcare or energy technology requires evidence in the relevant setting. A positive result at an earlier stage does not remove the requirements at later stages.

For animal or human work, competent review, appropriate consent where applicable, and welfare-sensitive endpoints must accompany any proposed intervention. The Holy Heart principle requires attention to suffering and the possibility of recovery without making scientific claims about subjective experience in every molecule or virus. The ordinary ethical and professional responsibilities for a domain remain in force. [1]

Questions for specialist review

The most useful review asks whether the proposed state is measurable, whether the comparison can separate it from simpler explanations, and whether the evidence supports the specified timescale. Neuroscience review should scrutinize cell specificity, retrieval confounds and neuronal-glial ordering. Molecular review should scrutinize isoforms, localization, binding partners and whether a perturbation changes more than one property. Microbiology review should scrutinize host identity and delayed ecological consequences. Material memory and molecular photoswitches

Lee, Mulder and Hopkins demonstrated architected mechanical networks whose beam stiffnesses were adjusted to produce learned mechanical responses. This is evidence for a trainable physical system with specified inputs and outputs. A release barrier controls persistence and rate; the energy difference between states determines available stored energy. Those are different quantities. [84, 85]

The Living Periodic Table is the author's functional taxonomy. Its 56 entries include cells, organelles, structures, processes, communities, devices and physical phenomena. Numbering is an index. The table is not a replacement for chemical elements, and adjacent placement does not establish a shared mechanism. The current manuscript definitions govern the companion's prose; original poster definitions remain visible in the glossary comparison. [1, 96]

Scoring analysis and interpretation rules

Before allocation, independent reviewers define distinct, nonduplicated scoring opportunities from domain evidence rather than from the ChristEye labels. The rubric accepts multiple defensible interpretations and justified requests for missing information. Each item earns one point when correctly addressed without an unsupported causal conclusion attached. Mere category naming earns none. Set a common response format and length limit, and request the most useful next question to discourage indiscriminate lists.

Scoring example: for an item distinguishing prompted from independent performance, “prompted success does not establish independent use” earns one point. “The prompt proves that the full experience was copied” earns zero and is recorded as an unsupported inference. An additional unsupported assertion elsewhere does not disappear because another item earned credit. In an illustrative six-item case with four justified distinctions and two omissions, the case score is 4/6; this is a scoring illustration, not a result.

Calculate the proportion earned within each case, then average those proportions equally within each participant. Compare participant-level scores with equal participant weights; do not pool all rubric items into an apparently larger sample. Cases and raters are repeated observations. Participant-clustered uncertainty addresses repeated cases; claims beyond the particular vignettes also require case-level modeling or evaluation on an independent vignette set.
